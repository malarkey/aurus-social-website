 At the heart of our work is the recognition that the UK is facing a significant and ongoing housing shortage. Reports suggest that around 1.3 million households are on waiting lists for social housing, while well over 150,000 children are living in temporary accommodation. This crisis is costing local authorities close to £2 billion every year, and responding effectively will require an estimated 2 million new social and affordable homes.

 As a specialist in social housing, we are proud to be playing an important role in meeting this need, while helping to secure a more sustainable future for the development of social housing.

 We’re doing that by making it a more attractive, reliable, and predictable asset class. Our share-based alternative to conventional buy-to-let investment offers fixed, regular returns without the risks of owning or managing properties.

 ## Disciplined underwriting

 Any opportunity we consider must meet strict underwriting standards before capital is deployed. We assess every project on a risk-adjusted basis. That includes:

 - Stress-testing financial assumptions
 - Assessing value for money and income security
 - Prioritising long-term contracted income, index-linked rents, insurance-backed payment obligations, and regulated tenant covenants
 - Testing asset quality and checking compliance standards
 - Determining the stability and governance of counterparties

 All assets are underwritten using independent RICS-regulated valuations, which must include multiple valuation scenarios and explicit disclosure of assumptions.

 <h2 data-size="3xl" data-variant="serif">Our focus on supported housing</h2>

 <p class="alt-lede">For many years, Britain has suffered from a shortage of good-quality housing. Too much of the country’s housing stock is old, poorly insulated, and prone to problems such as condensation, damp, and mould.</p>

 <div class="alt-columns">
 <p>We are focused on improving both the supply and quality of housing, and to making it accessible to the people who most need it.</p>

 <h2 data-size="xl">Sector challenges</h2>

 <p>The rate of housebuilding varies from year to year, but population growth and demographic change have created a pattern of consistent under-supply. Successive governments have set targets for new homes, and those targets have almost invariably been missed.</p>

 <p>Moreover, only a fraction of new housing is genuinely affordable, and most is aimed at homeowners rather than renters. At the same time, many smaller landlords have left the sector following tax and regulatory changes over the past decade. This has further restricted supply, leaving more tenants competing for homes and contributing to rising rents and affordability pressures.</p>

 <h2 data-size="xl">Social landlords</h2>

 <p>Local authorities and housing associations have worked hard to provide homes for people struggling to buy or rent. However, creating new homes is expensive, and few social landlords have the capital budgets to support major construction or conversions. That’s where we can help. Backed by private investors, we fund the acquisition of residential property, which we then lease to local authorities and other registered housing providers.</p>

 <h2 data-size="xl">General social housing</h2>

 <p>Our projects focus on general-needs social housing. While much media attention has been given to high-needs supported housing, there is an equally important, and often overlooked, need for general-needs housing with light-touch support.</p>

 <p>General-needs housing supports people who can live independently but may benefit from occasional help to maintain stability and manage daily life. Tenants typically include people on low incomes or those moving on from temporary or unstable accommodation. Their needs are low to moderate and do not require clinical care, intensive supervision, or specialist intervention.</p>

 <p>This form of housing represents the largest and most resilient segment of the social and affordable housing market.</p>
