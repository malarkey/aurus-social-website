  <p class="alt-lede">For many years, Britain has suffered from a shortage of good-quality housing. Too much of the country’s housing stock is old, poorly insulated, and prone to problems such as condensation, damp, and mould.</p>

main: |
 These issues can have a serious effect on people’s health, comfort, and day-to-day wellbeing. We are focused on improving both the supply and quality of housing, and making it accessible to the people who need it most.

 ## Sector challenges

 The rate of housebuilding varies from year to year, but population growth and demographic change have created a pattern of consistent under-supply. Successive governments have set targets for new homes, and those targets have almost invariably been missed. As a result, demand continues to outstrip supply across many parts of the country.

 Moreover, only a fraction of new housing is genuinely affordable, and most is aimed at homeowners rather than renters. At the same time, many smaller landlords have left the sector following tax and regulatory changes over the past decade. This has further restricted supply, leaving more tenants competing for homes and contributing to rising rents and growing affordability pressures.

 ## Social landlords

 Local authorities and housing associations have worked hard to provide homes for people struggling to buy or rent. However, creating new homes is expensive, and few social landlords have the capital budgets to support major construction or conversions.

 That’s where we can help. Backed by private investors, we fund the acquisition of residential property, which we then lease to local authorities and other registered housing providers.

block2: |
 ## General-needs social housing

 General-needs housing supports people who can live independently but may benefit from occasional help to maintain stability and manage daily life. Tenants typically include people on low incomes or those moving on from temporary or unstable accommodation. Their needs are low to moderate and do not require clinical care, intensive supervision, or specialist intervention.

 This form of housing represents the largest and most resilient segment of the social and affordable housing market.
